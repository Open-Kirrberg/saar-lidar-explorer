# 11 — Gardens and landscape of the Karlsberg residence, c. 1789

*Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: the Schlossgarten and its surroundings (Herzogsgarten east of the Corps de logis, the
terrace gardens, the Orangerie parterre, the Hofgärtnereien, avenues, ponds, the Hirschwürzloch, the Karlslust), the
design grammar of German court gardens in the 1780s, a planting palette, and concrete specifications for the model.
It adds to research/01 §1.2, §2.1, §2.3 (rows 16–44), research/02 §2.14 and §3.2, research/03 §4, research/06 §3.1 and
§3.7, and research/07 §1–2. Those sections are not repeated here.*

**Evidence tags:** **[D]** documented (source linked), **[D→]** documented elsewhere and transferred, **[I]** inferred
(reasoning stated), **[N]** period norm (treatise or comparable), **[U]** unknown or assumption, **[HMS]** from the HMS
2022 reconstruction (reference only, © Matzerath / Historisches Museum Saar, no frames or textures in the model).

**Units.** Period sources use the *toise* (1.949 m) and the French *pied du roi* (0.3248 m). Sckell (Munich, 1818/1825)
uses the *Fuß*; if it is the Bavarian foot, 1 Fuß = 0.2919 m. Metric conversions below give that range where it matters.

## 0. Key findings (short)

1. **There is an authentic garden plan of the Karlsberg, and it shows no parterre east of the palace.** It is in the
   Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, *Cod.icon. 180 h*, sheet 26r (MDZ `bsb00053415`, image 0024, Public Domain Mark).
   The sheet is undated and unsigned. Ralf Schneider attributes it to **Johann Georg Bernhard Petri**, who was
   appointed in 1788 to remodel the Karlsberg Hofgarten, and dates it to c. 1788 ([Schneider, "Entwurf zur Gestaltung
   der Carlsberger Gärten", schloss-carlsberg.de, archived 2023][sch-plan]; Schwan 2014 publishes the same find, see
   §7). [D]

   The plan shows these elements east of the Corps de logis (§2.1):
   - a **large open lawn** composed like an English landscape garden;
   - a **kidney-shaped belt walk** that starts and ends at the Corps de logis;
   - **clumps** of flowering shrubs, and single and grouped trees, including conifers;
   - **forest** belts;
   - **benches** placed at views;
   - small objects (sculptures or aviaries);
   - **four pavilions**.

   Schneider sums it up: "Hinweise auf ein ursprünglich vermutetes Rasenparterre finden sich nicht". [D]

   Model consequence: the hedged four-compartment parterre **TF-09 is the wrong garden type** for the 1788–89 design
   state.
2. **The 1789 landscape was open, not forested.** The 1791 *Pleine vue* (research/07, PV) shows meadows, fields, a
   vineyard, avenues and only small woods on the slopes below the palace. In 1760 the Buchenberg was unforested
   (research/01 §0). Petri's plan draws *Wald* only as belts around the lawn. [D]

   Model consequence: the viewer's forest is "today's canopy > 8 m, more than 40 m from residence features"
   (`scripts/prepare-terrain.py`). That rule wraps the palace in closed forest. **This is the largest visual error in
   the model.** [I]
3. **Several garden facts are new to this project** [D, Schneider web, citing the Landesarchiv Speyer building
   accounts and the 1793 damage lists]:
   - **Basin.** A basin with **two stag heads and a fountain** stood behind the palace, on the Corps de logis axis.
     It was built after 1783. A 1790 day-labour entry may record its demolition, so it **existed in summer 1789**.
   - **Benches.** The Orangerie cellar held **52 green-painted garden benches**.
   - **Rotunde.** A **Rotunde with a colonnade** was built in **1789** next to the 1788 Bildergalerie. This supports
     the HMS reading in research/07 C2.
   - **Round pavilion.** A **round pavilion with a double-stepped dome** stood near the Corps de logis (1785/86). It
     was **moved to the Karlslust in 1792** and only then called the "Eremitage". **It was not in the Karlslust in
     1789.**
4. **The Herzogskastanien were sweet chestnuts (*Castanea sativa*), not horse chestnuts.** de.wikipedia says the local
   people gathered the edible chestnuts ("Esskastanien"). This settles the [U] in research/02 §2.14. Separately, the
   1791 legend lists an "allée des marronniers sauvages", which is probably a horse-chestnut avenue (§3.3). [D]/[I]
5. **The Karlslust had more follies than the catalogue holds** [D, Saarpfalz-Touristik; Schwan via homburg1]:
   - a Chinese pavilion;
   - a Turkish pavilion;
   - a **Turkish tent**;
   - a **hay wagon (Heuwagen) folly**;
   - an aviary.
6. **The Paradestraße was a two-row avenue** ("zweihüftige Allee" in Petri's plan). The Holländische Gärtnerei had
   **two greenhouses facing due south with a central passage**, with rows of beds toward the palace [D Schneider on
   Petri]. The straight east axis avenue KB-12 / TF-11 appears in **none** of the period plans: LP06, K05 or Petri
   (research/07 §3). It is probably a later forest ride, so its 1789 tree rows are doubtful. [I]
7. **Period norms now have numbers.**
   - **d'Argenville** (1747 edn): allée width scales with length; double allées split half / quarter / quarter;
     banquettes 3–4 pieds; *parterre à l'angloise* = lawn pieces + flower plate-bande + a sanded path of 2–3 pieds.
   - **Sckell** (1825): drives 15–24 Fuß, walks 8–12 Fuß, footpaths 3–5 Fuß; avenue trees 20–30 Fuß apart in the row,
     Lombardy poplars 10–12 Fuß; side avenues 40 Fuß wide.

   §4 gives the tables. [N]

## 1. History and evidence

### 1.1 The three garden zones, and who designed them

The garden has three parts: the **park at the palace**, the **Karlslust** and the **Hirschwürzloch**. The park at the
palace shows "a strong baroque influence", while the Karlslust is "more an English landscape garden". There was
probably **no overall plan**, as there was none for the palace. Mannlich supplied designs for single garden scenes, and
F. L. von Sckell claimed to have worked twice for the duke ([de.wikipedia][dewp];
[parks-und-gaerten.de][pug]). [D]

**Petri plan versus "strong baroque influence".** The de.wikipedia statement predates the Petri plan find. The plan
shows that by 1788 the area behind the palace was being laid out (or re-laid out) in the landscape manner. Schneider
had already argued in 1999 that the ground behind the palace was deliberately asymmetric ([Schneider web][sch-plan],
citing *Die Gartenkunst* 2/1999). [D]

**The gardeners:**

| Person | Role at the Karlsberg | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Johann Ludwig Petri (1714–1794) | Zweibrücken court gardener. Designed Jägersburg (c. 1749/50), Pettersheim (c. 1760), the Zweibrücken Hofgarten (1762/63) and the Schwetzingen circle parterre (1753). Probably involved in the early planning | [D] [Schneider web][sch-intro]; [dewp]; [SSG press release][ssg-petri] |
| Ernst August Bernhard Petri (1744–1809) | Court gardener from 1766; Zweibrücken Eremitage 1776. At the Karlsberg he had overall charge of all garden matters: the pleasure gardens, "especially the Karlslust", kitchen gardens and greenhouses. Given a flat in the Orangerie in 1786 | [D] [de.wikipedia EABP][eabp]; Ulrich/Schwan 2008, pp. 22–24 |
| Johann Georg Bernhard Petri (1767–1853/54) | Trained under Sckell at Schwetzingen 1782–85, then studied English gardens (his 1785 plans of Kew, Richmond, Wilton and Syon are in the same BSB volume). From 1788 "Hofgärtner adjuncto", charged with remodelling the Karlsberg gardens in the English style. Later worked at Lednice-Valtice | [D] [de.wikipedia BP][bp]; [Schneider web][sch-intro] |
| Matthias Sckell | Court gardener at the Karlsberg (brother of F. L. von Sckell) | [D] [dewp] |
| Ludwig Reichard | Court gardener; his gardens / greenhouses are named in building accounts and in HMS ("Treibhäuser des Hofgärtners Ludwig Reichard"); LP06 45 "Gärtnerei Reichard + Kegelbahn" | [D] Schneider web fn 26 (LA Speyer B3 Nr. 2607, 2593); [HMS]; research/07 §2.6 |

**[I] The 1789 garden was therefore a hybrid**, typical of German courts in the 1780s:
- formal elements at the buildings (courts, the Orangerie parterre, the terrace garden, avenues, kitchen gardens);
- a young landscape garden on the plateau behind the palace, being remodelled in 1788–90;
- a sentimental-picturesque valley garden (the Karlslust) with exotic follies.

The closest parallels are Schwetzingen (a Petri baroque core with a Sckell landscape ring from 1777), Schönbusch (from
1775, Sckell from 1783) and Hohenheim (from 1776). See §3.

### 1.3 Other documented garden objects near the palace

- **Bassin with two stag heads (Hirschköpfe) and a fountain.** Built after 1783 behind the palace, **aligned on the
  Corps de logis axis**. Its traces were visible in the ground until about 20 years before Schneider wrote. A 1790
  day-labour list may record its demolition and backfilling, which would be linked to Petri's plan.
  - Sources: LA Speyer B3 Nr. 2595, B4 Nr. 2547, B3 Nr. 2611, via [Schneider web][sch-plan]. [D]
  - Position: on the axis, distance [U]. Form and size: [U].
- **Sculpture.** The group "**Bacchus auf dem Ziegenbock**" and a "**Florabüste**" in the Historisches Museum der
  Pfalz are said to come from the Karlsberg. [D, "angeblich"]
- **Garden benches.** 52 green-painted benches were in the Orangerie cellar (Schügens damage list). [D]
- **Aviaries (Volieren)** behind the palace are recorded in the accounts. [D]
- **Terrace garden S of the S wing.** Le Clerc 1790 shows a garden area **bounded by treillage** (Schneider fn 27). HMS
  shows a raised parterre on a rubble retaining wall, with an iron hoop fence, arched trellises, roses and sunflowers
  (research/06 §3.7). LP06 names it "44 Schloßterrasse". [D] / [HMS]
  - **[I, low]** It lies against the **Duchess's wing** (LP06: S wing = "Flügel der Herzogin"), so it is a candidate
    for the **"Lustgarten der Herzogin" (1784)**, catalogue UP-10.
- **Herzogsgarten.** LP06 49 is "Pavillon im Bereich des Herzogsgartens", a label E of the palace with no building
  drawn (research/07 §2.6). **[I]** "Herzogsgarten" is the period name of Petri's lawn garden behind the palace.
- **Dutch garden and greenhouses** (1779–83), Ananashaus (1781), Hofgärtnerei (1780–82): research/01 §1.2. A plan in
  the same BSB volume (sheet 25r, ["Anlage mit Gartenbeeten und Gewächshäusern"][bsb-25r]) shows a French-labelled
  forcing garden. It has *serres pour les ananas*, *serres de pêche*, hotbed frames (*couches*) for salads, beans,
  peas, cucumbers and melons, flower frames, a *ferme* and a *pavillon*.
  - Its site is **not identified** [U]. It is a good **type reference** for the Karlsberg Gärtnereien. [D→]
- **Vineyard.** PV "x. La vigne avec son logement"; LP06 30 Weinberg, a strip down the slope W/SW of the palace
  (research/07 §3.2). PV draws red-brown row bands on the right-hand slopes. [D]

### 1.4 Wider grounds

- **Approaches.** PV "8. la chaussée pour aller à Hombourg" is drawn as a long avenue of **narrow conical / columnar
  trees** across the foreground. PV "u. L'allée des marronniers sauvages" is not located. The 1782 Oberamt map shows
  the Homburg–Erbach road and a branch to the Karlsberg as avenues (research/07 §1.2; [Schneider, Fasanerie][sch-fas]). [D]
- **Water supply** (research/07 App. B):
  - PV S: an aqueduct from the **Lambsborn** spring, 1600 toises (≈ 3.1 km), "tombant dans un bassin aux grandes
    écuries";
  - PV v: a "fontaine par machine" feeding the kitchen and the barracks;
  - the Lambsbach water conduit of 1783–84 (research/01 §1.2). [D]
- **Karlsbergweiher.** PV "y. L'étang entre les deux montagnes"; LP06 32 draws it **larger (~170 m) and geometric**:
  a long rectangle with a rounded head and a small structure at the apex (research/07 §3.2; my reading of
  `sources/Karlsberg_Lageplan.gif`). [D LP06]
  - **[I, low]** The 1789 pond was a formal canal-like basin, not today's natural outline.
- **Carlsberger Mühlenweiher (Carlsmühle).** A dammed pond in the Lambsbach valley at the E edge of the grounds,
  **≈ 600 × 50 m, ≈ 3 ha** (BSB sheets 14r / 38r, mid-1780s) [D, [Schneider, Mühlenweiher][sch-muehle]]:
  - an **island reached by a bridge**, with a round structure in the centre and a fringe of **poplar-like trees**;
  - Schneider reads it as a "**Rousseau island**" after Ermenonville (1778), cf. the Saarbrücken Ludwigspark and
    Wörlitz;
  - a bay cut into the steep slope opposite, drawn like rockwork.
  - **Location:** the Lambsbach runs N–S at about E 384000 (OSM), so the pond lies **outside the viewer terrain crop**
    (E ≤ 383300). [I]
- **Karlslust** (research/01 §2.3 rows 33–40; research/03 §4.5; research/07 C3–C4, C15).
  - New in this file: a **Chinese pavilion**, a **Turkish pavilion**, a **Turkish tent**, a **hay wagon**, an
    **Eremitage** and an aviary ([Saarpfalz-Touristik][spt]); "ein chinesischer Pavillon, ein türkisches Zelt und
    erlesene Wasserkünste" (Schwan, [homburg1 2018][h1]). [D]
  - **Timing:** the Eremitage is the CdL round pavilion moved in **1792** (§1.2). For **1789** the Karlslust has **no
    Eremitage**, so the ring mound KL-12 had something else or nothing on it. [D] / [I]
  - **Hydraulics:** the Große Kaskade (10 × 1.5 m) was fed from the Großes Bassin. The spring in the tumulus niche
    (once **shell-lined**) and "the **fountain in the Schwanenweihern**" were both fed by the cascade system. Tumulus
    and dams were **faced with boulders (Findlinge)** ([dewp]). [D]
- **Herzogskastanien.** Planted under Karl II. August **above the Hirschwürzloch**, part of the Karlslust layout. They
  survived 1793 and were felled by storm Wiebke in 1990. "Die Bäume mit **Esskastanien**…" → *Castanea sativa*
  ([dewp]). [D]
- **Fasanerie / Amalienlust** (Erbach, outside the crop), from the 1777 and 1784 plans ([Schneider][sch-fas]). [D]
  - A W avenue opening to a half-round forecourt before the duchess's Schlösschen (≈ 23.5 × 13.7 m).
  - A **Rasenparterre** of compartments equal in width, flanked by **3 tree rows** (W side and along the axis) or
    **2 rows** (other sides).
  - A N–S **canal** with walks on both sides, widening to a **round basin** at the cross.
  - A grass strip on the E–W axis.
  - Square pavilions ≈ 5.5 m.

  This is the duchy's own formal grammar of the 1770s–80s [D→].

### 1.5 What the views say about 1789 vegetation

- **PV 1791** (`sources/Karlsberg_II.jpg`):
  - open grassland slopes with scattered trees;
  - a light-green rectangular field (garden or nursery) under the palace row;
  - dark tree rows and hedges in curving bands;
  - red-brown vine rows;
  - a long avenue of conical trees in the foreground;
  - only small dark woods (left) and a wooded ridge line at the far right.

  This is **not** a forested hill. [D, schematic]
- **Le Clerc 1790** (`sources/Schloss_Karlsberg_Leclerc.jpeg`):
  - the forecourt is **bare gravel / sand**;
  - **young round-headed trees** stand in a loose row in front of the guard houses, with a **post-and-rail fence**;
  - tall dark trees (poplar-like) stand behind the S-wing terrace at the right edge. [D]
- **HMS** (research/06 §3.1, §3.7):
  - an "Englischer Garten (Bernhard Petri)" E of the CdL with open meadows, loose clumps, serpentine gravel paths and
    an **octagonal pavilion**;
  - walled kitchen gardens with quartered beds;
  - tree rows along roads;
  - green July meadows. [HMS]

  This agrees with the Petri plan. [I]
- **Louisenhof 1768** (BSB 61r): the "herrschaftlicher **Buchenwald**" reached down into the Lambsbach valley
  ([Schneider, Luisenhof][sch-luise]). [D]
  - **[I]** So forest framed the plateau on the E, while the W and S slopes toward Homburg and the valley were open
    farmland and meadow.

## 2. Comparables: the 1780s transition from formal to landscape garden

Research/02 §1 already covers the buildings of these sites. Here only the garden grammar is taken.

| Site | Garden facts used here | Use for Karlsberg |
|---|---|---|
| **Schwetzingen** (Kurpfalz) | J. L. Petri's design of 1753 (the circle parterre); Sckell landscape park from 1776/77 **around** the baroque core, "in a way typical of Germany"; arboretum from 1777; Moschee 1779–91; Merkurtempel (a sham ruin, tuff) 1787–88; parterre sculpture by Guibal, Verschaffelt; waterworks on the Leimbach ([SSG][ssg-petri]; [en.wikipedia][schw]) | Same gardener family. A **formal core plus a landscape ring** is the expected 1789 state [D→]. Parallels: the aviary ring ↔ the Bärenzwinger, the Kastell ↔ the Schwanenweiher tumulus ([dewp]) |
| **Jägersburg** (Pfalz-Zweibrücken, the duke's 1776 residence) | Petri's inner park mixed strict baroque elements (**great canal, great basin, Rasenparterres**) with early English elements. Garden front on a **low terrace**; along the main axis a **Rasenparterre + large basin + canal + cascade** down to the Reiskircher Weiher; a "kleiner Thiergarten" fenced along the Erbach chaussée; a fenced "Lerchengarten"; a round "Hundsschwemme" basin ([Schneider, Jägersburg][sch-jbg]) | The direct local precedent for a **lawn parterre (gazon), not broderie** [D→]. It was being dismantled for the Karlsberg after 1777 [D] |
| **Fasanerie / Amalienlust** (Homburg-Erbach) | See §1.4: Rasenparterre in a frame of 2–3 tree rows, canal with flanking walks, round basin at the crossing ([Schneider][sch-fas]) | Formal module sizes; the duchess's own garden [D→] |
| **Tschifflik** (Zweibrücken) | Enclosure wall ≈ 4 km × **2.5 m**; terraces with central stairs and a cascade; an open-air stage; water features; Petri extended it "partly in the English style"; Fasanerie 1757–69 ([de.wikipedia][tschif]; [schloss-carlsberg.de Tschifflik][sch-tsch]) | Model for the Karlslust terrace-and-cascade scene and for Tiergarten walls [D→] |
| **Schönbusch** (Aschaffenburg) | From 1775 (Herigoyen), **Sckell from 1783**; ≈ 160 ha; two lakes (3 / 4.5 ha), a canal to a cascade, three artificial hills; Philosophenhaus 1785/87, Freundschaftstempel 1786/89, **Dörfchen 1788/89**, Rote Brücke 1789/90; vistas cut from old hunting rides; > 20 km of winding paths ([de.wikipedia][schoen]) | Exact contemporary. Hunting rides turned into **vistas**, and staffage hamlets [D→] |
| **Hohenheim** (Württemberg) | "Englisches Dörfle" planned from 1776: **> 60 miniature buildings at scale 1:4**; exotic plantings from 1778 ([de.wikipedia][hoh]) | Density and scale of follies; Karlsberg's follies were fewer and full-size [I] |
| **Ludwigsberg** (Saarbrücken, 1769–93) | Gardens by J. F. C. and H. L. Koellner; destroyed 1793; a Rousseau island in the Ludwigspark ([Schneider, Mühlenweiher][sch-muehle]; Paul 2009) | Regional rival; the sentimental repertoire (Rousseau island) [D→] |
| **Wilhelmshöhe** (Kassel) | Baroque cascade 1701–17 (Great Cascade 350 m); from **1785** a remodelling to the English style: aqueduct, Lac, Teufelsbrücke, Höllenteich, Steinhöfer waterfall 1793 ([en.wikipedia][wh]) | The same moment (1785–93) of turning baroque water axes into picturesque water [D→] |

**[I] What this means for Karlsberg 1789.**
- Formal elements stay where they serve buildings and traffic: courts, the Orangerie parterre, the terrace garden,
  avenues on roads, kitchen gardens.
- Pleasure grounds behind the palace become lawn, clumps and belt walks.
- The valley garden is picturesque and episodic.
- **Broderie parterres are out of fashion** by the 1780s. Even Jägersburg (1750s) used *Rasenparterres*. A
  *parterre à l'anglaise* (gazon coupé with flower borders) is the right formal type wherever a parterre is kept.

### 3.2 Age and size in 1789

- **Avenues and clumps were planted 1778–1788**, so they were 1–11 years old in 1789. Research/02 §2.14 (crowns
  4–8 m) and grammar `AVENUE.height1789 = 6` stay valid for avenues. [D→]
- **Petri's lawn plantings** of c. 1788 were young, but they used **retained older trees**: the CdL surroundings were
  already "Wald" in 1785/86, and the Louisenhof beech wood existed from before 1768. **[I]** So mix mature forest-edge
  trees (18–25 m) with young clumps (shrubs 2–4 m, trees 5–8 m).
- **Herzogskastanien** (planted after 1777) were ≤ 12 years old: 5–8 m tall [I].
- **Lombardy poplars** grow fast: 8–12 m after 8–10 years [N].

### 3.3 The "marronniers sauvages" question

The 1694 Académie dictionary used *marronnier* for any tree bearing marrons, including sweet chestnuts ([source
summary][marr]). D'Argenville's grand allée "de marroniers du Luxembourg" (1747 edn, Part I ch. V, footnote) is a
horse-chestnut avenue [N].

**[I, med]** "Marronniers sauvages" (wild, i.e. inedible) most likely means **horse chestnut** (*Aesculus*). That
distinguishes it from the edible Herzogskastanien (*Castanea*).

**Model rule:** "Kastanie" in the catalogue must say which species. The current `speciesFor()` maps every "kastanie"
to *Aesculus* (`model/src/site/trees.ts`), which is wrong for the Herzogskastanien.

### 4.3 Parterres (where they are kept: Orangerie, S terrace)

- **Type.** Use a **parterre à l'angloise** (gazon coupé). D'Argenville: it consists of "grands tapis de gazon tout
  d'une pièce, ou peu coupés, entourés d'une plate-bande de fleurs, avec un sentier ratissé de **deux ou trois pieds**
  de large qui sépare le gazon d'avec la plate-bande, & que l'on sable". [N]
  - This is the simplest parterre type and matches the Jägersburg / Amalienlust Rasenparterres. [D→]
- **Module.**
  - Lawn panels 8–20 m long, ratio 1:1.5–1:3, corners cut or rounded [N].
  - Sanded sentier 0.65–1.0 m.
  - Plate-bande **1.0–2.0 m** (3–6 pieds), raised 0.1–0.2 m with a box edge, planted with flowers, yew or box cones
    every 3.9 m, and standard roses between [N].
  - Cross walks **2.5–5 m** (5 t = 9.7 m is d'Argenville's cross walk for a grand parterre) [N].
- **Broderie** is not used: out of fashion by the 1780s, and no source mentions it [I].
- **Surface colours.** d'Argenville describes red-sanded sentiers, a **yellow-sanded** parterre ground, and **river
  sand** for the rest of the garden. Locally:
  - Buntsandstein grit gives a **pinkish to ochre** gravel [I];
  - yellow river or pit sand for fine walks [I];
  - courts in sand or gravel (Le Clerc: bare light sand-gravel) [D LC].

**Priority.** P1 = highest impact on the "wow" and on truth, P3 = nice to have. Visual payoff: ★★★ = changes the read
