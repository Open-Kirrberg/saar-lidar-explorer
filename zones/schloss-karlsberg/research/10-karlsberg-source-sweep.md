## 0. The five findings that change the model most

1. **The gallery length is "cent pas", not "hundert Fuß".**
   - Schwan 2012 note 47 says the German Mannlich edition mistranslates the French "cent pas". She equates 100 Schritt
     with 300 pieds, which gives **≈ 97 m** [D→ T5].
   - This supports the LP06 line of ≈ 95–105 m. It contradicts the "≈ 32 m" reading of the 1910 German text, and the
     HMS "≈ 40 m" range.
   - If a common *pas* of 2½ pieds is meant, the length is 81 m. That is almost exactly the catalogue's 82.9 m [D→].
2. **The gallery ends in a round Ionic "Tempelzimmer" with a pedimented portico, not a square pavilion.**
   - It is reached through the gallery, opens into the garden by three steps, and has large glass doors.
   - Its interior from 1789 is marble and granite imitated in paper (Schwan 2012, from the 1793 damage inventory) [D T5, T8].
   - The 2003 room list puts **Tempelzimmer (Granitsaal) → Kolonnade → Rotunde** in sequence [D R12].
   - The library pavilion **leaves the middle of the gallery** [D T1, T5].
3. **The 1785 Orangerie rotunda was a temporary timber-and-canvas building, demolished after the wedding.**
   - It stood in the Orangerie court and had a domed salon ≈ 60 Fuß wide, about 20 m in diameter and ≈ 16.5 m to the crown [D T1, T6].
   - KB-B13 must not stand in a 1789 state.
4. **The north wing (Duke) held the Silberkammer, a Tanzsaal and two Bildersäle.**
   - The "Salon Mannlich" was also there.
   - The south wing was put out to contract in 1782 and stayed a carcass (Rohbau) for some years [D T5].
   - The catalogue's EG Silberkammer and dining rooms in KB-B02 contradict this.
   - Mannlich and the 2003 room list put the **Großer Speisesaal on the upper floor of the kitchen wing** (KB-B05) [D T1, R12].
5. **The library was on the ground floor of the Sammlungsflügel.**
   - Four rooms in a row, 160 Schuh long in all ≈ 52 m, left of the Wachtstube.
   - The Naturalienkabinett and the Waffensammlung were on the 1st OG [D T5].
   - The catalogue currently has the armoury on the EG and the library on the OG.

### c.4 The 1785 Orangerie rotunda: section V20, read from the Schwan 2012 reproduction (Abb. 9)

The sheet can be used for a "Dezember 1785" scene only. It is CC0 at Darmstadt; **download request in §d**.

- **Structure:**
  - A steep **timber roof truss** (roughly triangular in section) is built over an inner vault, so the exterior is a tall pointed roof, not a dome [D].
  - Low lean-to side wings with flat roofs hold the booths and stages at both ends [D].
- **Inner dome:**
  - A pale **sky-blue vault with light clouds** (#9DB1E0-ish [N]), framed by a frieze of festoons and putti figures.
  - A central chandelier hangs on a long chain; T1 says amoretti held it [D].
- **Arcade:**
  - Round-arched openings between **pink/mauve columns or pilasters** under a cornice and false attic, hung with garlands.
  - Crystal chandeliers hang in the openings on the long axis [D].
  - T6: 16 Ionic columns, probably only painted; coffered soffits; vases and putti on attic pedestals [D].
- **Ambulatories:**
  - Piers painted **green and gold** with statues in niches; arched bays [D].
- **Lighting:**
  - The sheet's note says the red dots around the archivolts and the cornice are the **lampions** [D].
  - T6: thousands of hidden lamps light the dome as if it were open sky [D].
- **Proportions:**
  - On the sheet, crown height ÷ salon width ≈ 0.8 [D→].
  - This is consistent with Mannlich's 60 × 50 Fuß and Schneider's 20 m / 16.5 m.

### d.4 KB-I53: Corps de logis massing check and the concealed stair (2026-11-02)

The question from §b.1 (KB-B01 row): does the T1 phrase "einstöckiges Wohnhaus mit Mansarden" argue for a lower
Corps de logis than the model's (Sockel + EG + Beletage + mansard, centre pavilion with its own hipped roof to
366.2 m and a lantern to ≈ 367.8 m)? No geometry was changed for this note.

| Source | What it says about the CdL | Date / kind | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 intro (editor Stollreither 1910, after Molitor T4 1885) | "einstöckiges Wohnhaus mit Mansarden", projecting wings enclosing a large court | second-hand, ~95 years after the fire; describes the **Closen/Louisenhof core** (1760/61) that T1 p. 365 says was "aufgebaut und außen verschönt" | D (text) for the core; I for 1789 |
| T1 p. 365 (Mannlich, eyewitness) | the Closen country house kept its character but was built up and embellished | first-hand memoir | D |
| LC 1790 (Le Clerc vedute, research/07 §1.2) | **2 full storeys + mansard** on the wings and the CdL; 7-axis centre pavilion with a **higher hipped roof and lantern/spire** | eyewitness view, idealised but architectural | D |
| PL plate (research/07) | 2 storeys + mansard with dormers; CdL pavilion roof + Dachreiter | decorative, symmetrised | D-> |
| HMS (research/08 §4) | eaves 356.0 ± 0.8, side ridge 362.7 ± 1.0, centre hipped roof **366.2 ± 1.2**, lantern ≥ 367.8 (spire missing) | 2022 reconstruction, numbers only (© Matzerath / HMS) | HMS |
| Model (catalogue v2, KB-B01) | base 344.8; storeys 1.4 + 5.2 + 4.6 → eaves 356.0; side ridge 362.72; 7-axis garden centre raised 3.5 m → 366.22; lantern 1.6 m → 367.8 | — | D LC + HMS |

Reading:
1. In 18th- and 19th-century German, "Stock" / "Stockwerk" usually counts the floors **above** the ground floor, so
   "einstöckig" can mean ground floor + one upper storey, i.e. the two full storeys LC 1790 shows [I, usage].
   Read that way, T1/Molitor and LC agree.
2. Even read literally (one storey + mansard), the phrase describes the **1760/61 core's character**, not the
   enlarged 1789 house. Mannlich himself says the core was built up (T1 p. 365) [D]; the catalogue dates the
   enlargement 1778-82 (§b.1).
3. LC 1790 is the only period view of the 1789 state and is first-hand. It shows the higher centre roof and the
   lantern; the HMS centre ridge 366.2 m matches it and the model reproduces it within 0.05 m [D LC; HMS].
4. Nothing places the centre pavilion lower: no plan, section or account gives its height [U].

**Decision (KB-I53, 2026-11-02):** keep the CdL massing (two full storeys on a Sockel, mansard, 7-axis centre
pavilion with its own hipped roof to 366.2 m and a lantern). Add to KB-B01's notes the reading that T1/Molitor's
"einstöckig" describes the core's character (or counts the upper floor only). Re-open only if Schwan 2010 (§d.2 no. 4)
or a hi-res LC 1790 scan shows a lower centre.

**Concealed stair (T5):** Schwan 2012 says the Duke started in "winzigen verkürzten Appartements" with a secret
stair hidden in a cupboard. That describes the **early phase** (before the state apartments) [D], and no source places
the stair [U]. If it is modelled, it is an interior detail: a narrow winder stair behind a jib door in a cabinet next to
the Duke's private apartment (EG garden side, research/05 §e.1), `confidence: 'low'`, tag `D T5 existence; position I`.
It is a separate interior item, not part of KB-I53.
